What changed on 1 August 2026

ISC2 updated the CCSP exam outline effective 1 August 2026. The six domains kept their names, but the content and weighting shifted. If you are studying from older material, these are the gaps to close.

Key changes
  • AI and machine learning security integrated across the outline. Two entirely new subtopics were added: 1.6 Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning, covering threat detection, data source validation, SOAR, ethical concerns, and regulatory requirements; and 2.9 Data protection for AI and ML, covering dataset and model privacy alongside validation and verification. AI considerations also appear throughout the remaining domains.
  • Domain 4 decreased from 17% to 16%.
  • Domain 5 increased from 16% to 17%, making Cloud Security Operations jointly the second heaviest domain.
  • Expanded supply chain and pipeline security, including secrets management, Infrastructure as Code scanning, and Software Bill of Materials.

Domains 1, 2, 3, and 6 kept their existing weightings.

How this material was generated

For transparency, here is the brief used to produce the study material and the question sets.

Study material. For each domain, write material covering every subtopic in the ISC2 exam outline, in the plain and direct register a professional courseware author would use. Explain each concept clearly enough for someone new to it, and give a concrete worked example for every concept rather than definitions alone. Map each section to the corresponding chapter of the Official Study Guide, 3rd Edition, and state plainly where the book predates the current outline. Add exam-trap callouts flagging the distinctions candidates most often confuse.

Question sets. Write practice questions in the style of the real exam: scenario-driven, testing applied judgement rather than pure recall, mixing straightforward knowledge checks with longer scenarios that present a business context and ask what the security professional should do next. The task was framed as writing items that prioritise the best or most appropriate answer among several plausible options, not merely correct against obviously wrong.

  • Four options per question, exactly one correct.
  • Distractors must be plausible and reflect genuine misconceptions, such as confusing data owner with data custodian, mixing up SOC 2 Type I with Type II, or misapplying shared responsibility boundaries. No throwaway or obviously absurd options.
  • Cover the key frameworks and standards throughout: ISO/IEC 27001, 27017, 27018, NIST, CSA Cloud Controls Matrix and STAR, GDPR, OWASP, and the shared responsibility model across IaaS, PaaS, and SaaS.
  • Vary the question stems: what to do first, what is the best control, which is the correct sequence, and straight definition checks.
  • Do not test the same concept more than twice, and cover subtopics broadly rather than clustering.
  • Give a rationale for every question explaining why the correct answer is right and why the leading distractor is wrong, shown whether the user answers correctly or not.
  • Label each question with its subtopic so weak areas can be tracked.
  • Interleave question order across subtopics rather than grouping them, and distribute correct answers evenly across the four option positions so there is no pattern to exploit.

Interface. Two tabs per domain, study material and questionnaire. Each question has its own submit button and reveals the outcome immediately. Options stay unlocked so answers can be changed and resubmitted, a progress counter tracks attempts and correct answers, and a reset control clears everything for a retake.
